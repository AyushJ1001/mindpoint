# Indian privacy and accessibility obligations for the Mind Point LMS

Reviewed on 11 September 2026. This note supports product planning. It is not legal advice. It assumes that Mind Point is a private Indian business, sells non-accredited courses to adults in India, and will host student and faculty posts in a course Q&A area.

## Decision

The LMS can launch as an adult, non-accredited course platform, but its specification should include the controls below from the first release. Some are required now. Others prepare the product for the main Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act) duties that commence 18 months after the November 2025 Gazette publication.

1. Build one privacy system for itemised notices, purpose-specific consent, consent withdrawal, access, correction, erasure, nomination, grievances, processor contracts, retention, and breach response. Do not wait until the main DPDP duties commence in May 2027 to add these data-model fields and workflows.
2. Treat course operations and marketing as separate communication purposes. Record channel and purpose consent independently. Essential course messages must be identifiable as service messages. Promotional SMS and voice calls must use the TRAI commercial-communications system.
3. Adopt WCAG 2.2 AA as the product acceptance target, map it to IS 17802, and test the learner, faculty, assessment, Q&A, feedback, and certificate flows with keyboard and assistive technology. Provide captions or transcripts, text alternatives, accessible documents, sufficient contrast, visible focus, error identification, extra-time accommodations, and non-drag alternatives.
4. Make the Q&A feature an actively moderated intermediary feature. Publish terms, a privacy policy, prohibited-content rules, and grievance-officer details. Keep evidence and registration records for the periods described below. Build removal, appeal, and lawful-request workflows.
5. Define a record-retention schedule by record type and purpose. India does not impose one general retention period for the academic records of a private, non-accredited short-course provider. The schedule must combine the current 180-day intermediary and CERT-In periods, the future one-year DPDP floor for specified logs and processing records, and longer periods required by tax, payment, contract, or litigation needs after counsel confirms them.
6. Require an 18-or-over declaration at registration and give staff a process for suspected minors. Do not collect identity documents merely to prove age unless a risk review justifies it. If the product later admits minors, it needs a separate child-data design before launch.
7. Describe awards as "course completion certificates." Do not call them degrees, recognized postgraduate diplomas, licences, or accredited qualifications. Keep public certificate verification minimal and separately consented.

This matters if learner discussions, accommodations, counselling-related courses, or uploads contain health information. |

## Privacy requirements in the specification

### Current baseline

The [SPDI Rules, 2011](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_CEN_45_76_00001_200021_1517807324077&filename=GSR313E_10511%281%29_0.pdf&type=rule) currently sit under section 43A of the [Information Technology Act, 2000](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/13116/1/it_act_2000_updated.pdf). They cover a narrower class of sensitive information than the DPDP Act. The specification should still inventory all personal data and flag sensitive fields. Passwords, payment data, physical or mental health details, medical records, biometrics, and sexual-orientation information need the SPDI consent, disclosure, transfer, security, and grievance controls now.

The current privacy policy should state what personal and sensitive information Mind Point collects, why it collects it, how it uses and discloses it, and its security practices. Collection should be necessary for a lawful purpose, retention should last no longer than the lawful purpose requires, and the learner should have review, correction, and consent-withdrawal routes. Processor and cross-border arrangements need a data-flow register rather than being hidden in implementation code.

The [CERT-In directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf) add operational duties that are easy to miss in a product specification. Security requirements need an incident register, a six-hour escalation path for listed incidents, an India-resident 180-day log store, synchronized clocks, and a named point of contact. These are security logs, not permission to keep every learner record indefinitely.

### DPDP-ready design

The [DPDP commencement notification, G.S.R. 843(E)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf) places the main obligations in a future stage. The final [DPDP Rules, 2025](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf) use the same schedule. As of the review date, it would be wrong to describe the May 2027 fiduciary duties as already in force.

The launch design should nevertheless meet them. The notice needs an itemised account of the data, each purpose, and the linked service, plus direct methods to withdraw consent, exercise rights, and complain. Consent must be specific, informed, unambiguous, and withdrawable as easily as it was given. Store the notice version, purpose, action, time, channel, and withdrawal state.

The data model needs access, correction, erasure, grievance, and nomination requests. Mind Point remains responsible for processors, so contracts with Clerk, Convex, email, payment, analytics, file-storage, and video providers need security and deletion terms. The rules call for encryption or comparable protection, access controls, access logs and monitoring, backups, processor security clauses, and technical and organizational controls. The breach workflow must notify affected people and the Board without delay, followed by the prescribed detailed Board report within 72 hours.

The future rules require at least one year of personal data, traffic data, and processing logs for the listed statutory purposes, followed by erasure unless another law requires longer storage. This one-year floor should not become a blanket permanent-retention policy. The DPDP Act also requires erasure when consent is withdrawn or the specified purpose is no longer served, subject to legal retention.

Public certificate verification needs its own privacy choice. A verification page should accept an unguessable certificate identifier and return only the minimum confirmation fields. Publishing a learner's name, grade, email, assignment history, or Q&A activity should require a separate, recorded choice that can be withdrawn without revoking the underlying certificate.

## Accessibility requirements in the specification

The [RPwD Act](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_DL_64_796_00001_201649_1547618167086&filename=a2016-49_act.pdf&type=actfile) defines private establishments within its framework, prohibits disability discrimination unless the act is a proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim, and requires government and private service providers to comply with accessibility rules. The [Supreme Court's 8 November 2024 judgment](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf) confirms that sections 40 to 46 contemplate mandatory standards, but invalidated Rule 15(1)'s method of incorporating mostly advisory guidelines. The latest official instrument found was DEPwD's [July 2026 draft amendment for non-negotiable ICT standards](https://depwd.gov.in/en/draft-rules-on-non-negotiable-accessibility-standards-for-information-and-communication-technology-sector/). It was still a consultation draft, not a final replacement rule, on the review date.

That gap does not justify an inaccessible LMS. The current [IT Rules, updated 10 February 2026](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/550681ab908f8afb135b0ad42816a1c9.pdf) require intermediaries to take reasonable measures to make services accessible. The Indian government also lists [IS 17802 Parts 1 and 2](https://depwd.gov.in/en/acts/) as its ICT accessibility reference. The product should use [WCAG 2.2 AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) as its acceptance target, maintain an IS 17802 mapping, and run an accessibility regression suite. This is a product-risk decision, not a claim that WCAG 2.2 AA is the present text of a binding Indian private-LMS rule.

Accessibility is part of the content model, not only the page shell. Each activity needs accessibility metadata and alternatives. Videos need synchronized captions and transcripts. Audio needs transcripts. Images and diagrams need meaningful text alternatives. PDFs and ebooks need tagged, selectable, correctly ordered content or an HTML alternative. Timed quizzes need approved extensions and pause rules. Assignments, feedback, Q&A, notifications, and certificate downloads must work without a mouse and with screen readers. The specification should include an accommodation-request route and a published accessibility contact.

## Q&A, moderation, and records

Student questions and faculty replies introduce user-generated content. The [current consolidated IT Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/550681ab908f8afb135b0ad42816a1c9.pdf) define a social-media intermediary as one that primarily or solely enables user interaction. A course platform may instead be a general intermediary. Classification depends on the final service, so counsel should confirm it before launch. The ordinary intermediary controls are still the right design baseline.

The product needs terms that prohibit unlawful, infringing, privacy-invasive, impersonating, harassing, and malicious content. It needs reporting and moderator queues, a published grievance officer, complaint acknowledgement within 24 hours, resolution within seven days for the general current rule, faster handling for specified categories, and an appeal record. When content is removed under the rule, preserve it and associated evidence for 180 days. Retain user registration information for 180 days after account cancellation. Do not expose private questions to other learners, and use role-based access for faculty and moderators.

The Q&A composer should warn users not to post medical records, payment details, identity documents, or confidential third-party information. Courses in psychology or therapy must keep educational discussion separate from diagnosis, treatment, emergency response, or a clinical record unless Mind Point deliberately enters a regulated care workflow after a separate legal review.

## Communications and sales

The LMS should classify every outbound message as operational, assessment, security, certificate, or marketing. Email and in-app course notices do not fall within TRAI's telecom preference regime in the same way as SMS and voice calls, but their personal-data processing will require a valid DPDP basis once the main provisions commence. Capture course-operations consent in the enrolment notice and make optional marketing a separate unticked choice. Withdrawal from marketing must not block password resets, security alerts, purchased-course access, deadline messages the learner selected, or receipts.

For SMS and voice, follow TRAI's [consolidated TCCCPR text](https://trai.gov.in/node/3199). The implementation needs registered sender identity, approved headers and templates, recorded consent where applicable, preference and revocation handling, and suppression lists. Do not reuse an operational template for promotions.

Paid online courses also engage the [Consumer Protection E-Commerce Rules, 2020](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/sites/default/files/E%20commerce%20rules_0.pdf). Course pages and checkout should show the provider identity, contact and grievance details, full price, access period, prerequisites, faculty status, refund and cancellation terms, certificate conditions, and whether the course has any recognition. Completion automation must not contradict advertised grading, resubmission, attendance, or faculty-approval terms.

## Adult-only and qualification boundary

The [DPDP Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/22037/2/a2023-22.pdf) defines a child as anyone under 18. An adult-only product avoids the child-processing workflow only if it enforces that boundary. Use an 18-or-over declaration, adult-only terms, a support escalation for suspected minors, and an account-blocking path. If minors become part of the roadmap, first add verifiable parental consent and the statutory restrictions on tracking, behavioral monitoring, and targeted advertising. Those substantive child-data duties commence with the main DPDP duties in May 2027.

Mind Point may issue factual course-completion certificates. Section 22 of the [UGC Act, 1956](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_CEN_9_9_00004_195603_1517807319834&filename=195603.pdf&type=actfile) reserves degrees to authorized institutions. The [UGC Distance Education Bureau FAQ](https://deb.ugc.ac.in/FAQ) states that its online degree and postgraduate-diploma framework applies to recognized higher education institutions. The specification must therefore avoid claims that a Mind Point certificate is a degree, recognized postgraduate diploma, professional licence, academic credit, or accredited qualification. Any course tied to a regulated profession, university partner, or external accreditor needs its own approval and wording review.

## Retention policy to approve before implementation

| Record | Proposed specification rule |
| --- | --- |
| Account profile and enrolment data | Keep while the account or course-access purpose continues. On closure, apply the 180-day intermediary minimum if applicable, the future DPDP one-year processing-record rule, and any separately documented legal need. Then erase or de-identify. |
| Q&A content removed for a rule violation | Preserve the removed content and associated records for 180 days, or longer if a court or authorized agency requires it. Restrict evidence access. |
| Security and ICT logs | Keep a rolling 180 days in India now. Design for the future DPDP one-year minimum for prescribed personal data, traffic data, and processing logs. |
| Quiz attempts, assignment submissions, grades, feedback, and completion evidence | Keep through grading, appeals, certificate issuance, and the disclosed verification or dispute period. Set a finite period after counsel aligns contract and limitation requirements. Do not retain identifiable drafts or telemetry without a purpose. |
| Certificate record | Keep the minimum immutable issuance, status, and revocation record for the disclosed verification period. Separate this internal record from optional public display. |
| Consent and notice history | Keep enough evidence to prove the applicable notice, purpose, affirmative action, withdrawal, and suppression state for the relevant processing and dispute period. |
| Consumer and privacy grievances | Keep through resolution and the documented dispute period. Restrict access because complaints may contain sensitive data. |
| Invoices, payment and GST records | Store separately from learning activity. A covered registered person must retain GST accounts and records for 72 months from the due date of the relevant annual return, with longer retention for specified proceedings under [CGST Act section 36](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content-page/explore-act/1000306/1000001). Confirm applicability with tax counsel. |

## Counsel checks before launch

Product counsel should confirm four points against the finished service and the state where Mind Point operates: whether the Q&A implementation is an intermediary or social-media intermediary, the final retention periods for contractual disputes, whether any course title or subject engages a professional regulator, and whether final non-negotiable ICT accessibility rules have replaced the draft. Tax counsel should confirm GST record obligations. These checks may change compliance details, but they do not change the product controls chosen in this report.

# Curriculum authoring prototype

## Scope

Operate-mode prototype for Administrators and assigned Faculty. The route compares three structures for building a Draft Curriculum version and its assessments without touching live data.

## Direction contract

THESIS: Make publication risk the organizing principle. The selected "Release desk" keeps the curriculum, active editor, and blocking checks in one working view instead of hiding readiness in a final wizard.

OWN-WORLD: Preserve Mind Point's warm neutral ground and calm lavender controls. Use compact workhorse interface type, crisp bordered work areas, restrained status colors, and one terracotta warning channel.

STORY: An author sees the Draft Curriculum version, fixes the highest-impact blocker, edits the exact activity that caused it, checks Student preview, then hands a ready version to an Administrator for publication. Publishing never activates Enrollments.

FIRST VIEWPORT: A narrow product rail anchors the left. The main area opens on a readiness header and a blocker ledger, while the curriculum outline and selected Reading editor remain visible beside it. Selecting a blocker moves focus to the matching field. The signature interaction is this blocker-to-field jump.

FORM: Release desk, ranked third among seven grounded structures and assigned as the lead by surface seed `3c0d68f6`. The comparison also includes the first-ranked Outline desk and fifth-ranked Journey canvas, switchable with `?variant=A`, `B`, or `C`.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance

## Constraints

- Faculty may author and request review. Administrators publish.
- Course storefront state and Curriculum publication state remain separate.
- Published manifests are immutable. Structural changes create a new version.
- Required activities cannot publish with failed rights or accessibility checks.
- Publication and Enrollment activation are separate actions.
- The prototype is synthetic, in-memory, responsive, keyboard operable, and clearly marked as throwaway.
